# Lexical language reference

Target: [OCR J277 specification version 3.1, section 3c](https://www.ocr.org.uk/images/558027-specification-gcse-computer-science-j277.pdf), plus the project rules below. These project rules are not claims about OCR requirements.

- Identifiers consist of ASCII letters, digits and underscores and compare case-insensitively; retain original source spelling. `123abc` is a valid identifier. CamelCase and Hungarian-style prefixes are recommendations, not requirements. Other digit-leading and underscore-only boundary cases remain open. A bare identifier cannot contain embedded expressions, interpolation or escape sequences; a backslash in `name\thing` produces `unknown-symbol`.
- Reserved words, alphabetic operators, and the built-in names `print`, `input` and casts use entirely lowercase or entirely uppercase spelling. Mixed-case spellings, including `For`, `fOR`, `aNd` and `DiV`, are identifiers if they satisfy the identifier grammar, not keywords, operators or built-ins. `for` and `FOR` are keywords.
- All built-in members and annotations retain their exact documented case, such as `length`, `substring`, `writeLine` and `close`. `byVal` and `byRef` are exact-case project extensions, not documented J277 section 3c constructs.
- Alphabetic operator examples: `12MOD5` is `12`, `MOD`, `5`; `xMODy` is the identifier `xMODy`; `MOD5` is `MOD`, `5`. The general boundary rule and its interaction with mixed-case identifiers remain open.
- The established warning applies when lowercase `mod` and uppercase `DIV` appear together in the same snippet. Warning category and wording are undefined.
- Mixed-case `print`, `input` and cast names are identifiers if valid; they do not produce the former mixed-case warning.
- Type declarations are optional; their syntax is not specified.
- Recognize documented multi-character comparisons such as `==`, `!=`, `<=` and `>=`. `//` introduces a comment, but not inside a string. Comments are retained as trivia.
- Punctuation tokens have distinct kinds: `LPAREN` and `RPAREN` for `(` and `)`, `LBRACKET` and `RBRACKET` for `[` and `]`, `LBRACE` and `RBRACE` for `{` and `}`, plus `COMMA`, `COLON` and `DOT`. Curly-brace tokens are a project extension; tokenisation does not establish an OCR construct using them.
- Classify lexical errors, including `unterminated-string`, `unknown-symbol`, `invalid-number` and `invalid-keyword-case`. Provide source locations or spans and messages, with room for future call-site or stack-frame metadata; no stack trace is required. Recovery, warning representation and exact messages remain open.
- OCR illustrates straight double-quoted strings and single-quoted characters. The test assumptions also exercise empty and multiline strings, escaped quotes, EOF comments, UTF-8 with an optional BOM, CR/LF/CRLF and newline tokens. These are implementation choices rather than settled OCR rules. Strings can contain Unicode; identifier letters are ASCII. Input positions count UTF-16 code units, with one-based lines and columns and exclusive ends.
- Signs are separate operator tokens. Leading zeros and missing decimal zeros generate warnings; normalized numeric text is kept separately from source spelling. Numeric separators are accepted as numeric text. Context-dependent identifier validation, inference and overflow are outside the lexer. Alphabetic numeric suffixes are treated as identifiers where the name is valid, leaving the `1e3` exponent/name ambiguity open. A decimal with digits on both sides of the dot followed directly by an alphabetic name suffix, such as `1.2abc`, is a lexical error rather than an identifier; the same text is allowed inside a string. The established `MOD`/`DIV` tokenization is unchanged. Other decimal/name boundaries remain open.
- Scanning stops on a lexical error, preserving prior tokens and comment trivia; warnings do not halt. Unsupported escape sequences and unsupported operator runs are errors. Further numeric grammar, declaration syntax, source boundaries and recovery remain open.
- `open`, `newFile`, `step`, initialized arrays, `const`, `real`, `bool` and `random` are documented in J277. Do not assume the older guide's `openRead` and `openWrite` are J277 constructs.

Open conflict: identifier case insensitivity versus keyword recognition requires preserving source spelling for classification; whether a case-equivalent spelling that is a keyword can refer to that identifier is unresolved. Parsing and name resolution are outside lexer scope.
